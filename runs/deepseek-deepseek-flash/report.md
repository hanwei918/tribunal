### Advocates replayed

For 22 of 22 graded cases the rider and driver outputs were **reused from another run** and were not regenerated by the run that produced these results: only the judge was called for those cases. Every figure in this report is therefore the judge's behaviour on fixed inputs rather than a measurement of the whole pipeline. The brief and both advocate prompts were checked against what that run recorded, and against the model, before any case was accepted.

The advocates came from `runs/deepseek-deepseek-flash`.

Those calls cost 1,363,060 tokens, billed to the run that made them and not to this one. No figure in this report includes them.

A ruling counts only when **all four** fields agree. The per-field figures above are there so a reader can see which field a model actually fails on; they are not alternative scores.

Reported as a contrast only, and not as a result: `ruling` and `amount_sgd` alone would give **21/22 = 95.5%**. Case 207 is `ESCALATE` with both money fields `null` and a referral instructing the reviewer to refund the rider's SGD 4.00 in full, so a two-field grader awards it full marks while a real rider does not get their money back. The gap between this line and the four-field line is the size of that mistake on this run.

## How this was checked

`tests/test_grade.py` feeds each case's own `ground_truth` block back in as though a perfect judge had produced it and requires 29/29 on every field, five escalations, a rate of 17.2%, and precision and recall of 1.0. It then perturbs one field at a time — a ruling, an amount by 0.05, a `null` driver compensation replaced by 0, case 207's referral dropped, a referral added where the key expects none — and requires each perturbation to fail on its own field and on the four-field match, without disturbing the other fields' counts. No model is involved at any point.
