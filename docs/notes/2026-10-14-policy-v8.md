# Policy version 8: version 7 with Ryde's answers of 2026-10-14

**Status.** Written 2026-10-14. Version 8 is `policy/v8/`, and it is **version 7
plus two of the readings version 7 had left open**, so it is a later policy on the
same base rather than a second one. Version 7 stays exactly as it is, under
`policy/v7/`, with every run that was made at it.

## The three questions and the three answers

On 2026-10-13 the owner sent Ryde three questions in the track group, and Ryde
answered on 2026-10-14 at 10:49 SGT:

The questions are given here in substance; the answers are Ryde's own words.

1. **Q.** v2.1 6.2 makes a route-deviation ruling an AI ruling only if the
   confidence is 0.80 or more **and** the deviation is 2 km or less, else the case
   is escalated -- but DISP-001's deviation is 4.3 km and its stated outcome is an
   AI partial refund of 3.44. Which wins?
   **A: "Escalate."**
2. **Q.** Where does the no-show free wait start for a scheduled trip the driver
   reached early?
   **A: "From driver arrival."**
3. **Q.** Is surge applied to the excess distance fare?
   **A: "No - the excess distance fare refund is calculated at the base per-km
   rate, without surge."**

Those are the readings this note has called C1, C2 and C3 since the draft of
2026-10-13.

## What version 8 changes

Two things, and only two.

**The 2 km limb of v2.1 6.2 applies (C1).** `route_deviation.json`'s
`thresholds.ai_ruling_max_deviation_applied` is now `true`, so a route deviation
of more than 2 km is escalated rather than ruled, DISP-001's 4.3 km included.
`POL-RD-25`'s rule now states the limb as part of the clause instead of holding it
in a switch, and its note records the answer. The deviation's own measurement,
the 1 km tolerance, the refund matrix and the excess distance fare are untouched.

**The free wait runs from the driver's arrival (C2).** `no_show.json`'s
`thresholds.free_wait_starts` now names `trip_record.arrived_at`, a new
`thresholds.free_wait_starts_from` states the same reading as a rule, and
`POL-NS-21`'s rule and note are restated on the driver's arrival: the clock runs
from the arrival whether the trip is scheduled or on demand, so the free wait no
longer starts at the scheduled time when the driver reaches the pickup early.

## What version 8 does not change

**C3 was already so.** Version 7 read the excess distance fare at the base
per-kilometre rate with no surge, which is what Ryde confirmed. `POL-RD-24`'s note
now says the reading was confirmed rather than proposed; no number and no rule
moved.

**C5 was not asked.** What "partial" and "full" refund of the excess distance
fare each pay -- both of which version 7 reads as the whole excess distance fare,
because "partial" describes the trip fare and "full" the excess distance fare --
was not put to Ryde, so it is unchanged.

**C4 and C6 to C18 stand as they are.** The dispute value is the amount the filer
asks back (C4); the optional fraud flag and the 0.95 bar it raises (C6); the
optional 30-day no-show counter (C7); the fee on the record (C8); the ruling
vocabulary (C9); what confidence is (C10); a verbal notice with no recording
(C11); rounding to the cent (C12); the driver-no-show deadline on an on-demand
trip (C13); which trip the repeat counter counts (C14); the fraud flag on a
no-show (C15); the arrival test and the reach of its accuracy filter (C16); which
distance governs a deviation (C17); and the kinds of traffic event a reroute needs
with the types each admits and the normalisation rule (C18).

**Versions 1 to 7, and every run made at them, are unchanged.** Nothing at
version 7 or below reads `policy/v8/`: the version-8 files are reached only when a
run or a brief asks for `policy_version >= 8`, and the version-7 brief rebuilds to
the bytes every committed version-7 run recorded.

## The keys that move

Two version-8 sets reuse the version-7 case files and differ from them only in
their keys, because only expected outcomes move.

**`dev-v8`** (the 40 `dev-v7` cases, 5001-5020 and 5051-5070): **two** of its 40
entries change, both from `PARTIAL_REFUND` to `ESCALATE` --

| case | deviation | was | is |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5010 | 2.3 km | `PARTIAL_REFUND` 2.12 | `ESCALATE`, no amount |
| 5015 | 2.6 km | `PARTIAL_REFUND` 1.87 | `ESCALATE`, no amount |

**`ryde-v8`** (the organisers' two samples, 902 and 903): **one** entry changes.
Case 902, DISP-001, at a deviation of 4.3 km, goes from `PARTIAL_REFUND` 3.44 to
`ESCALATE` with no amount, and its rationale records that Ryde's answer supersedes
the outcome the sample itself states. Case 903 is unchanged: `DRIVER_COMPENSATION`
of 5.00, from the driver's arrival at 08:43 the free wait ends at 08:48 and the
cancellation is at 08:51, after it, which is the same answer version 7 gave.

Both sets' other entries are copied **byte for byte** from the version-7 keys:
every field of every other entry, and every field of the two changed entries
except the ruling, the two amounts, the referrals, the clause list and the
rationale.

**The free wait moves no key.** The dev-v7 and sealed-v4 generators derive a
no-show answer from the driver's arrival and assert that the scheduled-time
reading agrees, so answer 2 changes no expected outcome in either key -- it
changes what the brief says about *why* the clock started where it did.

## The sealed set

**`sealed-v4` stays a version-7 measurement.** It is 30 cases run once, at policy
version 7, on 2026-10-14, and that run reads 30 of 30 four-field agreement. It is
**not** re-keyed, none of its files move, and no part of it is re-run.

Under the version-8 rule exactly **one** of its 30 cases would change: case 6009,
at a deviation of 2.4 km, would become `ESCALATE` rather than the ruling its
version-7 key states. That is not a figure. Version 8's 2 km gate produces that
escalation **before the judge is asked** -- it is read off the brief's authority
block and cites POL-RD-25 -- so a version-8 run of 6009 would be a gate firing,
not a judgment about the case. The other **29 were not run at version 8** and
nothing here claims what they would have produced.

## Where the version-8 text lives

`policy/v8/` holds `general.json`, `no_show.json` and `route_deviation.json`,
copied byte for byte from `policy/v7/` and edited at fifteen JSON paths and
nowhere else, with `policy/v8/amendments/README.md` recording that no amendment
has been approved and `policy/v8/CHANGES.md` listing every edit. Version 8 is a
separate base, so its three files are read whole: `policy/v8/` is version 8, and
any later version on that base would apply the amendments under
`policy/v8/amendments/`.

## 2026-10-15: Ryde's position on following v2.1

On 2026-10-14 the team put C15 to Ryde: Tribunal reads v2.1 6.3 as setting a
0.95 bar when a party carries a fraud flag, while v2.1 6.2 makes a no-show
ruling AI-final, and the two readings part company on case 903. On 2026-10-15
the organisers relayed Ryde's position: teams need not follow v2.1 strictly and
may use their own policy for testing. The organisers also confirmed that v2.1
reached the track group on 2026-10-13 only because a team asked for it.

**What this settles.** C15 is Tribunal's own, stated reading rather than an open
question: 903 stays escalated, and it stays the one known divergence from Ryde's
sample. Nothing follows from the relay for the policy itself. No clause changes,
and no version 9 is made: `policy/v8/` is exactly what it was, and version 8
stays the latest version.

**What is still open.** The question about publishing v2.1 and the samples in
the public repository has not been answered, and this note does not treat it as
settled.
