Digital Native Track - Multi-Agent Autonomous Dispute Resolution System (Ryde)

1. Project Overview

Tribunal Agent settles two of the brief's dispute categories, route-deviation fares and no-show charges, under Ryde's own Dispute Resolution Policy v2.1 with Ryde's answers of 14 October 2026 (our policy version 8). Its users are Ryde's support agents and reviewers, and the rider and the driver in each dispute. Every ruling names the evidence and the policy clause it rests on, and a case the system cannot decide goes to a person with the money held.

Live demo (no account, no key): https://tribunal.edgeone.dev/
Source code: https://github.com/hanwei918/tribunal

2. Real World Scenario Insights

Ryde's challenge statement names the pain points: millions spent on human support hours, typically 24-72 hours per dispute, different agents reaching different conclusions on similar cases, and users who feel unfairly treated leaving the platform. Tribunal answers each one: a decision in minutes; the same evidence brief and the same clauses for every case; one judge that rules four times, once with the parties swapped; and a written reason in plain words for both parties (the prototype shows the notice; it sends nothing). A safety flag, a dispute over SGD 50 or a route deviation over 2 km never gets an AI ruling, as v2.1 and Ryde's answers require.

3. Comprehensive Solution Design

The flow. A dispute is filed. Code builds one evidence brief from the trip record, the GPS trace, the chat and the fare, with plates, phone numbers and e-mail addresses masked and no model used. The Rider Advocate Agent and the Driver Advocate Agent argue from that same brief. An authority gate sends a safety flag, a value over SGD 50 or a deviation over 2 km to a person. A citation check in code drops any point whose evidence id or clause id does not exist. The Judge Agent rules four times, once with the parties swapped: three drafts must agree, and the confidence must clear v2.1's bar, 0.80 from SGD 10 up and 0.95 whenever a party carries a fraud flag. Otherwise a person decides, with the evidence, both sides' points and every draft in one packet. Every step is written to a hash-chained log, so any case can be replayed without calling the model.

The architecture. Python 3.9 to 3.13, standard library only. Every model call goes to Tencent Cloud TokenHub (DeepSeek, Singapore); the site is hosted on Tencent EdgeOne Pages; support staff can ask about any case through a read-only WorkBuddy connector. The code, the tests and the documents were written in WorkBuddy, one task per session, 134 sessions in all.

How prompts drive the AI. The two advocates share one prompt template, rendered with two stance files, and the code checks that the rendered prompts differ only in the stance. Every claim must cite an evidence id and a clause id. The judge prompt tests the claims against the record and caps its confidence by the weight of the evidence that decides the case. Distances, waiting times, fares and refunds are computed by code and handed to the agents; a draft whose amount is not read off the brief, or that cites a clause that does not exist, is voided.

4. Business Value

Measured on dev-v8, 40 development cases the team tuned against, and re-derived from the saved runs by python3 -m eval.v8_report --report:
- a median of 153.1 seconds per case, against the brief's 24-72 hours;
- a median model cost of 0.0390 USD per case, so 1,000 disputes a day cost about 1,170 USD a month in model calls (0.039 x 1,000 x 30), before hosting and human review;
- 28 of 40 cases (70%) decided with no person needed;
- in 33 of 34 cases the draft written with the parties swapped agreed with the panel;
- an expected calibration error of 0.097.

A sealed test set, run once at policy version 7, agreed in 30/30 (Wilson 95% [88.6%, 100%]). On Ryde's two samples, the 4.3 km route deviation (902) goes to a person under the 2 km limit, as Ryde answered; the no-show (903) goes to a person under our reading that a fraud flag needs 0.95 confidence, where Ryde upholds the fee. Ryde lets teams use their own policy, and this reading is stated.

Limitations

Every case except the organisers' samples 901, 902 and 903 is synthetic. The development figures come from data the team tuned against, and the sealed set was written by our own development assistant with no person reviewing its key, so neither is an independent benchmark. Names are not masked. Fare and surge disputes and image analysis are out of scope. Version 8 has no appeal clause yet; appeals exist only in the version-6 operations layer. Throughput limits are computed, not measured.
