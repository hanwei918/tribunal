What it costs.
How it rolls out.

The version-8 figures, what they would mean at a platform's volume, and a careful way to switch it on. The volume and the reviewer minutes below are assumptions, and are labelled so.


Per case, measured.

The 40 version-8 development cases (dev-v8). They are synthetic, so a pilot on real disputes would set the real figures.

Model cost
$0.039

Median per case: 0.0390 USD, deepseek-flash through Tencent Cloud TokenHub in Singapore.

Time to a decision
2.6 min

Median 153.1 s; p90 299.4 s. A person takes 24-72 h today (Ryde's brief).

No person needed
70%

28 of 40 decided by the AI; 12 went to a person with the file prepared.


At 1,000 disputes a day.

Arithmetic on the figures above. The daily volume and the 15 reviewer-minutes per case are assumptions, not measurements.

Model cost
$39 a day

1,000 x 0.039 USD; about 1,170 USD over 30 days.

Reviewer time saved
175 h a day

The 70% the AI decides is 700 cases; at an assumed 15 minutes each, about 22 reviewers on 8-hour shifts.

Still for a person
300 a day

The 30% that need a person, each with the evidence brief, both sides' checked points and the drafts already in the file.


Three steps to switch it on.

A proposed rollout. The prototype has run none of these steps on a live platform.

  1. 01

    Shadow

    Tribunal rules on past and current disputes on the side; agents decide as they do today, and the two are compared. No money moves.

  2. 02

    Assist

    The ruling and the notice arrive as a draft in the agent's queue; a person approves or changes each one.

  3. 03

    Decide within limits

    AI rulings take effect only inside the version-8 limits: below SGD 10 final; from 10 to 50 only at a confidence of 0.80 or more (0.95 with a fraud flag); above 50, a safety flag or a deviation over 2 km, a person decides. A switch that turns automatic rulings off exists in the version-6 operations layer; bringing it to version 8 is on the roadmap.


How it connects.

Plain files in, plain files out.

  • In: one case file per dispute -- the dispute, the trip record and GPS trace, the in-app messages and calls, the fare -- in the shape of the organisers' samples. Ryde's platform is mocked by these files.
  • Out: a ruling record with the amount, the clauses and the evidence cited, and the ruling's explanation in plain words for the rider and the driver. It is generated, not sent: the platform would send it and move the money from its own systems.
  • For reviewers, in the version-6 operations layer: the queue, the escalation packet and risk signals as command-line tools, and a read-only WorkBuddy MCP connector with three tools.
  • Cost of a run: six model calls per case by design -- two advocates and four judge drafts -- and two when the gate stops a case before the judge.

Money safety.

What stands between a model and a refund.

  • Distances, waiting time and fares are worked out by code, not guessed by the AI. Even a person can only pick from the amounts code allows.
  • Every point an advocate makes must cite evidence and a clause that exist; code throws out the rest before the judge reads anything.
  • The judge rules four times, once with the sides swapped, and three must agree. Otherwise the money is held and a person decides.
  • A safety flag, a value over SGD 50 or a route deviation over 2 km goes to a person before the judge is asked.
  • Every question, answer and check is logged, and any change to the log shows.

Rules and data.

What Tribunal follows, and what it does not claim.

  • Policy: version 8 applies Ryde's policy v2.1 with Ryde's answers of 2026-10-14, written as numbered clauses. Where Tribunal reads a clause its own way, the case page says so (case 903).
  • Personal data: the current code masks vehicle registration marks, telephone numbers and e-mail addresses before a model reads a case; names are not masked. A Singapore deployment would be subject to the PDPA; no compliance claim is made.
  • Appeals: the version-6 operations layer offers one appeal within 30 days to a different person; bringing it to version 8 is on the roadmap.
